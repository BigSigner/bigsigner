# Working on BigSigner

BigSigner is an air-gapped Bitcoin signer for a portrait 480×800 touchscreen. Seeds stay in memory. The browser emulator and the Raspberry Pi panel run the same `App` object.

`SPEC.md` is the product spec. When its "Resolved decisions" section disagrees with the rest of that file, the resolved section wins. [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) is the map of the code. Tutorials in `docs/` describe what a person does with another wallet. They are not installed on the device.

## Screen

The panel is 480×800. The title bar is 88 pixels and does not scroll. The page margin is 20. Colors and the button rules live in `src/bigsigner/ui/canvas.py`, `.cursor/rules/button-colors.mdc`, and `.cursor/rules/button-labels.mdc`.

Buttons are the brighter blue, `(24, 92, 184)`. Dark navy, `(18, 36, 58)`, is only for a button that is currently deselected in a choice, such as the passphrase keyboard modes. A disabled button is grey. The title bar, toasts, and the boot screen may stay navy. Those are not buttons.

Button labels use Title Case. The words a, an, the, and, or, to, of, for, with, from, and in stay lowercase unless they are the first or last word. A label that is not a sentence uses the same Title Case. OK, QR, and SD stay in capitals. A technical token such as xpub keeps its own spelling, as in xpub only. Product terms keep their spelling: SingleSig, MultiSig, Native-Segwit, Taproot. Native-Segwit is written with a hyphen. The passphrase keyboard modes stay `abc`, `ABC`, `.?123`, and `#+=`.

There is no horizontal scrolling. Long text wraps at a space or after a comma. A space is dropped at the break. The comma stays on the line it ended. The message being signed, and a file or folder name, may break at any character. A value with no space or comma, such as an xpub, breaks at the character that would overflow. Fingerprints, xpubs, descriptors, and other technical values use the monospace font. A full Bitcoin address is monospace groups of four characters, four groups on each centered row. The first and third group on a row are black, and the second and fourth are grey, so every odd group is black and every even group is grey. Spaces pad the last row to 16 characters so the groups line up in columns. The address list on a wallet stays one line per address.

The title bar text is 32px. Body paragraphs and buttons are 36px, a little larger than the title. On Bitcoin Keys, the section labels, the fingerprint name, the hex, the seed summary, and an external key's kind and account are 32px. The fingerprint name on a key card is centered and breaks after the second word. A Bitcoin Wallets card lists the fingerprints under the title, monospace and separated by commas, breaking after a comma, and puts a key icon or an eye icon to the left of each fingerprint name. On the seed key, external key, and wallet screens the name breaks the same way, with a key icon for a seed or an eye icon for an external key on the left, lined up with the fingerprint icon. On a SingleSig or MultiSig wallet each key, with that name, the fingerprint, and the account, sits in a white card. Tapping from the top of that card down to the Account row asks Go to Seed Key? or Go to External Key?. Confirm opens that key, and Back returns to the wallet. Deleting the key returns to the wallet when the wallet remains, and to Bitcoin Keys when the wallet is removed too. The seed key screen's fingerprint line, the first-word line under it, the seed sentences, and the buttons follow the title and button sizes above. The first-word line uses the seedling icon and reads First Word: followed by the first word in lowercase. Derive an Extended Public Key opens a choice of Native-Segwit SingleSig, Native-Segwit MultiSig, or Taproot SingleSig. That choice opens Derive Key, which starts with an account selector and then the external-key details, without Delete. Derive a BIP85 Child Seed sits under Create a Copy of This Seed with a Passphrase, or under Create a Copy of This Seed with a Different Passphrase when a passphrase is set. The label breaks after BIP85. Number of Words is a dropdown of 12 words or 24 words, defaulting to 24 words, followed by an index. The child is English BIP39 from m/83696968h/39h/0h/{words}h/{index}h. 83696968 is the BIP85 purpose. The child has no passphrase. After it is added, Back returns to Bitcoin Keys, and the notice is BIP85 child seed was added successfully. A seed that is already loaded shows This seed is already loaded. For a seed without a passphrase, Show and Export sit side by side under the passphrase note. Show opens the privacy warning and then Show Seed; Export opens the backup warning directly. For a seed with a passphrase, keep Show/Export and its choice screen with Show Seed, Show Passphrase, and Export Seed to SD Card. Export warns about a physical backup and wiping the card, then writes the words separated by spaces. The seed key's last button is Delete Seed. Export to SD Card sits under Show Full Details there, and the button that creates or opens the single-signature wallet follows it. On an external key, Export to SD Card sits under Show Full Details. The button that creates or opens the singlesig wallet follows it, and Delete Key follows that. On an external key, the type, account, derivation path, and extended public key use that fingerprint size. The path and the key preview are each centered on the next line, and Delete Key follows the other buttons. Text cut off because it does not fit ends with …, one character, the same mark as an xpub preview. Addresses on a wallet opens Receive, selected first, and Change. Each row is the index, a period, and the address, cut with … when it does not fit. The list derives a window of rows around the scroll position and drops rows farther away. Addresses Tools finds an index or verifies an address in ranges of 100. On a Receive or Change address, Sign Message sits under Export to SD Card and the page scrolls to show it. It is enabled for a Native-Segwit SingleSig wallet and opens Manual Entry, Scan QR Code, or Load from SD Card. The message is signed with that address. Other wallets show the button disabled, with a note that signing messages is supported only in Native-Segwit SingleSig wallets. A message cannot be longer than 1024 bytes. A signmessage command whose content is longer is refused, and the text box does not take another character past 1024. A signmessage command is refused there, including one typed after any leading spaces or newlines. Continue stays disabled, and a red note says to use Sign Message on the wallet's main screen. On the wallet, a centered Descriptor: label sits under Addresses, with Show and Export side by side under it. Sign Transaction sits under that row. It is disabled for a watch-only wallet, with a note that signing transactions is not available in watch-only wallets. It opens Load Transaction. After the file or QR payload is assembled, a progress screen with no title shows the current check and a bar that fills before Transaction Signing or Error loading PSBT; touches do nothing there. A PSBT file may be base64, base43, or the BIP 174 binary form that begins with psbt and the byte 0xff. A file or QR that is not a PSBT stays a popup and names what it is. A signature already on an input is checked, and every signature on a multisig input is checked. A signature that does not match is refused. A PSBT that fails a check opens Error loading PSBT. The reason is red, and OK returns to Load Transaction. When that PSBT spends from another account, the screen says to go back to the wallet screen and change the account index. A transaction with more than 100 inputs, or more than 100 outputs, is refused. A PSBT that passes opens Transaction Signing. Amounts are satoshi counts written as BTC strings: pad to at least nine digits and place the point eight digits from the right, with commas in the whole part. Do not divide a satoshi amount by a float. The screen names the spending wallet. Under the account line, Transaction Id is a chevron, closed at first, and opening it shows the transaction id in four centered monospace rows. The screen lists warnings, shows each output that is not change, the fee and fee rate, the locktime, the change outputs, and then the inputs. Sign Transaction signs the one loaded seed. A multisig note above the sign buttons counts the fewest signatures on any input and how many more the threshold needs. A multisig wallet with more than one loaded seed shows one button per seed. A seed that has already signed every input has that button disabled, with a grey note naming the key. Continue under those buttons stays disabled until one of them is used. While a signature is computed, that button reads Signing… and is grey, and taps do nothing until it finishes. When signing ends, pending touch events are flushed from the device and a short gap starts after that frame is painted, the same way Searching addresses ignores stale taps so a press during the wait cannot click the next screen.

Every tap action starts a fixed 250 ms input guard after its resulting frame is displayed. Compare the current monotonic time, not the input event timestamp. Discard queued touches after that frame; ignored input and scroll redraws do not restart the guard. Slow work deferred past the first frame, such as address search and Signing…, starts a fresh guard when its result is displayed. The browser also discards taps queued against the previous frame. Back from the signed result shows the PSBT that was loaded. The signed result offers Show QR Code, Export to SD Card, and Back to Wallet near the bottom. Back to Wallet clears the back stack and opens the spending wallet (Bitcoin Wallets behind it). Sign Message (BIP137) sits under Sign Transaction. It is enabled for a Native-Segwit SingleSig wallet whose seed is loaded. A watch-only wallet shows it disabled, with a note that it is unavailable in watch-only wallets. Any other wallet shows it disabled, with a note that signing messages is supported only in Native-Segwit SingleSig wallets. The Brightness control dims or brightens the whole screen, not only the QR. It is there so a camera reading that screen is not blinded when the panel is too bright. Black stays black. Every QR a camera is meant to read uses that control the same way. The side menu keeps its own size.

## Browser emulator

Tests do not start it. They construct `App`, call `render`, and tap.

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src python -m bigsigner.bundle
python3 -m http.server 8780 --directory web
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:8780/ . Run the bundle command again after changing Python, then reload the page. `web/bundle.json` and `web/external-assets/` are gitignored. That folder holds `pyodide/`, `pillow/`, `embit/`, and `qrcode/`, pinned by the bundle command. Do not type a real seed into the emulator. The hash supplies `seed`, `descriptor`, and `network`. The SD card is the files stored in the browser. `BIGSIGNER_INITIAL_SEED` is for tests and the panel, not this page.

## Tests

```bash
pytest
```

`PYTHONPATH=src python tests/panel_device.py` syncs to seedsigner.local and drives the panel. Do not run it unless asked. Screenshot tests write `artifacts/screenshots/<test name>/` and an `index.html` gallery. Check every screenshot you take for text that overflows its area. If the overflow runs in a direction that area cannot scroll, ask whether to fix the code before changing it. A flow that stands in for scanning a QR code mounts the payload on the in-memory SD card and clicks the plus button, then Load from SD Card, then the file. The browser camera is not a stand-in for that card.

## Inject a seed

`python -m bigsigner.rpc` writes one command to the pipe named by `BIGSIGNER_RPC`, as it was typed. The default path is `/tmp/bigsigner-rpc`. It does not check the command. `addseed` is one command: the line is that word and then the mnemonic. A 13th, 19th, or 25th word is the passphrase, and that word does not have to be in the word list. The panel checks the line. It loads a good seed without the manual-entry screen and refuses a bad one. On the boot screen a new seed waits until Continue on Remove SD Card. After that, the seed key opens. `tap x y` is a finger down and a finger up. `tapdown x y`, `tapmove x y`, and `tapup x y` are those phases. A line with an unknown command word is refused.

From the computer, `bigsigner-os/docs/scripts/rpc.sh` sends the line over SSH to the panel that is already running. It does not stop that process. A seed that is already loaded is left as it is. The app on the device has to already contain `rpc.py`. The panel opens `/tmp/bigsigner-rpc` when it starts. `BIGSIGNER_RPC=off` disables that.

Do not run either command unless asked. Do not pass a real seed. The Abandon test mnemonic is the one the tests use.

## External keys

A bare key expression is not stored on the first screen. Checks for a path that is not supported, a duplicate, or a seed mismatch open Error loading key (or Error loading wallet, from the wallets page). OK returns to Bitcoin Keys or Bitcoin Wallets.

A wallet descriptor loaded from Bitcoin Keys opens a choice screen instead. A descriptor that is not supported opens Error loading key, with no mention of Bitcoin Wallets. A singlesig descriptor offers Both Wallet and Key (the wallets-page import) or Only the Key (the key expression). A multisig descriptor offers Load Both Wallet and All Keys, then a menu of `<fingerprint> <first 10 characters of the xpub>…` in descriptor order, then Load Only Selected Key. A multisig wallet has at most 15 participants (m-of-15). More than 15 keys in a descriptor, or selecting a 16th key on Native-Segwit Multisig, is refused.

When the checks pass, Load External Key is two screens. The first is a red Warning and a Show Extended Public Key Details button. That button keeps the same gap above the bottom edge as a one-line button. The second shows the type, account, derivation path, and a format menu (`xpub` or `tpub`, the SLIP-132 form when the path has one, and the full key expression). Only Continue on that second screen stores the key. Back from the details returns to the warning. Back from the warning leaves the key unloaded.

## Fingerprint names

`animal_name` in `src/bigsigner/names.py` names a fingerprint. The name is calculated, not chosen, and not stored as an editable nickname. The first three bytes are one integer. That integer is divided across 982 adjectives, 51 colors, and 346 animals from unique-names-generator. Adjectives are at most 9 letters, colors at most 9, and animals at most 10. Those are the shortest limits for which the lists still cover every value of three bytes. The name ends with the last two hex characters of the fingerprint. The worked example is in [docs/fingerprint-names.md](docs/fingerprint-names.md).

Cards on Bitcoin Keys: Seeds stay in `session.key_counter` order. SingleSig External Keys and MultiSig External Keys are separate sections, each shown only when it has a card. Each external key is its own card. Within a section those cards are ordered by fingerprint nickname, then Native-Segwit before Taproot, then account number.

## Licenses

Bundled notices are in `src/bigsigner/licenses.py`, shown from About → Licenses, and summarized in `LICENSE`. A new library or copied asset needs its notice in all three places. The word lists in `names.py` are MIT-licensed unique-names-generator data, not BigSigner originals.
